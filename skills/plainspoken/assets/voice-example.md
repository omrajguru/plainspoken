# Voice: Example author

- **Reader:** backend developers who know the basics of databases and HTTP; explain anything specific to our stack before relying on it.
- **Person:** "I" for my own work and views. "We" only for decisions the team made together.
- **Formality:** conversational, with contractions. Plain words over clever ones.
- **Openings:** start with the result and its number, then explain how we got there.
- **Certainty:** clear positions. "I think" and "probably" only where the doubt is real, and never more than one hedge in a sentence.
- **Punctuation:** no em dashes. Parentheses are fine. Semicolons rarely. No exclamation marks.
- **Spelling:** US English.
- **Words I use:** in practice, it turns out, the catch is, worth it, the boring option.
- **Words I never use:** awesome, super (as an intensifier), ninja, rockstar, best-in-class.
- **Name:** not used.

## Sounds like me

> Our p95 latency dropped from 900 ms to 120 ms, and almost all of it came from one missing index.

> I could have added a queue here, but there was not much reason to. The jobs are small, they finish in under a second, and a queue would be one more thing to monitor.

> The catch is that this only works while the table fits in memory. Past that, you need a different approach.

## Notes

Keep the work as the subject, not the person who did it. Describe what was built, why, and what we learned, and let the reader decide whether it was good.
